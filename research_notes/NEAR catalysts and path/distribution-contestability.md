# Distribution contestability — REVSHARE and the SwapKit pipe

*Exploratory. Twin: `data/derived/contestability.json`. As of 2026-11-12.*

## Takeaway

The largest Intents distribution risk is not a peer matching vault security — it is **affiliates inside multi-venue embeds** being paid to prefer another settlement rail **without raising the user’s fee**. THOR’s REVSHARE is built for that. SwapKit already monetizes Intents beside THOR/Maya/Chainflip.

## Arithmetic that matters

- SwapKit: **38.2%** of 1Click volume; explicit NEAR buyback **~$159** vs partner fees **~$3.53M**.
- Traceable captured-revenue loss if SwapKit exits: on the order of **~2%** of Intents captured revenue (explicit line). The larger hit is to the **volume-share narrative** and any escrow-linked economics not attributed by channel.
- near.com captive proxy: ~**25%** of Q3 Intents volume with stated 100% fee direction.
- Contestability proxy: SwapKit-class share vs near.com share — high contestability means capture is rented.

## What NEAR can do without a take-rate war

Attachment creep, near.com mix (~$1M/year per pp), confidential+attestation. What fails: forcing SwapKit 50/50 (exit risk on 38% of 1Click) or ignoring REVSHARE’s ability to re-rank quotes inside the same wallet pipe.

## Proprietary punch

1. Contest the **embed**, not the vault bake-off.
2. Traceable SwapKit exit loss on explicit buybacks is small; the multiple’s volume story is not.
3. Capture share can rise on a SwapKit exit while the investment case weakens — score both.
4. REVSHARE subsidizes affiliates without cutting user fees — race-to-zero sits on NEAR’s buyback line.
5. Raise captive mix and attachment before renegotiating Commercial Agreements.

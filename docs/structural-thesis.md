# Distribution owns what Intents settles

*Exploratory / post-freeze prop research (as of November 2026). This section is **not** a pre-registered test: figures are synthesized from primary fee docs, DefiLlama methodology, Nansen Q3 2026, Bitwise product disclosures, and the project’s 30-day window ending **2026-11-04**. It answers what structural facts matter for a NEAR Intents investment thesis beyond what a blockchain RPC can show.*

The structural facts that matter for NEAR Intents cannot be read from a blockchain RPC alone: who prices the user, who keeps the gross fee, how confidential execution rewrites both product and attestation, and whether regulated wrappers change float. Over the 30 days to **2026-11-04**, Intents held about **55.7%** of peer-set cross-chain volume at a blended **15.3 bp** take, yet only about **5 bp** of volume reached NEAR revenue wallets — partners kept at least **1.81×** that captured slice on the visible 1Click half alone ([DefiLlama NEAR Intents](https://defillama.com/protocol/near-intents); [NEAR Intents Fees](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/fees)). Aggregate wallet inflows of **418.8K NEAR (~$2.01M)** reconcile to **100.9%** of DefiLlama Intents revenue, so the capture *total* is real; roughly **59%** of buyback-wallet NEAR arrived as confidential-escrow unshields whose fee stream is unattested by design ([DefiLlama near-intents adapter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DefiLlama/dimension-adapters/master/fees/near-intents/index.ts); [Confidential Intents](https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/market-makers/confidential-intents)). Confidential Mode and near.com’s default privacy expand a partner-proof fee vertical while concentrating trust in a **seven-validator** private shard and a TEE bridge ([How Confidential Intents Works](https://www.near.org/blog/how-confidential-intents-works); [NEAR Blockchain](https://www.near.org/blockchain)). Bitwise’s US spot ETF (NRR) sits near **$55.9M** net assets (**1.2%** of NEAR mcap) after a strong launch and subsequent fade — additive demand and a competing narrative, not a substitute cash-flow for Intents ([SoSoValue US NEAR spot](https://sosovalue.com/assets/etf/us-near-spot); [Bitwise NRR launch](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/newsroom/the-bitwise-near-etf-nrr-launches-as-first-spot-near-etp-in-the-us)). The investment-relevant stack is therefore distribution contracts, confidential trust assumptions, and discretionary buyback policy — not L1 gas or headline venue volume. Distribution owns the margin; escrow economics are unattested; the ETF stack is small; the confidential trust set is permissioned.

## Partners price the user; the protocol takes a pip

A NEAR Intents user pays a layered price: wallet or affiliate `appFees`, a 1Click infrastructure take (or Confidential Infrastructure Fee), solver execution spread, and a **0.0001% (1 pip)** protocol fee on `intents.near` ([NEAR Intents Fees](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/fees)). Solvers trade as principal and keep bid-ask economics; Intents Technology relays quotes and is never the counterparty ([Solver Terms of Use](https://partners.near-intents.org/legal/solver-terms)). Only the fraction that lands in three named wallets — `fefundsadmin.sputnik-dao.near`, `1csfundsadmin.sputnik-dao.near`, and `buybacks.multisignature.near` — is what DefiLlama counts as holders revenue for buybacks, and that NEAR is **held, not burned** ([DefiLlama near-intents adapter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DefiLlama/dimension-adapters/master/fees/near-intents/index.ts)). Public marketing defaults (50/50 with partners; near.com directing 100% of fees to a NEAR wallet) describe policy, not the SwapKit-heavy reality: Ledger, Trust, and Tangem routes pay listed partner fees in full on chain, while explicit buyback lines on those channels are often zero ([Fee Configuration](https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/distribution-channels/1click-api/fee-config); [Fees](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/fees)). On $1K–$50K 1Click swaps in the window, realized cost exceeded listed fees by about **8.5 bp** on average — solver and execution economics that never appear in DefiLlama “revenue.”

That ownership is sticky because wallets own attention and negotiate Commercial Agreements that override Schedule 1 defaults ([1Click Terms of Use](https://docs.near-intents.org/security-compliance/terms-of-service)). SwapKit alone routed about **38.2%** of 1Click volume in the latest 30-day block — roughly a fifth of one-sided Intents — while contributing almost nothing to explicit buyback fees ([Fees](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/fees)). Traceable revenue loss if SwapKit exited is only about **2%** of captured Intents revenue; the ceiling including unattributable escrow-path buyback inflows runs much higher, which is itself the risk. Nansen’s post-Q3 update puts lifetime fee capture near **16.4%** and trailing 30-day capture near **28.4%** (down from **30.5%** prior): NEAR’s claim is real and rising from a low base, still the minority of gross, and already soft on the margin ([Nansen NEAR Q3 2026](https://nansen.ai/post/near-q3-2026-report)). Volume is whale-sensitive — swaps ≥$10K are **3.4%** of count but **77.1%** of 1Click volume — and Intents barely touches the NEAR asset itself (NEAR on one side only **3.5%** of 1Click volume; the venue was a net exit for NEAR in the window). The only token link is fee → discretionary buyback. Governance retains discretion; “no specific mechanism is guaranteed” ([Evolving NEAR Tokenomics](https://www.near.org/blog/evolving-near-tokenomics)).

The cleanest observable NEAR-side lever inside public 1Click is buyback-fee *attachment*: the share of volume carrying an explicit buyback entry rose **23.6% → 43.0%** across three consecutive 30-day blocks as explicit buyback take rose **1.83 → 4.08 bp**, while SwapKit’s volume share stayed roughly flat ([DefiLlama adapter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DefiLlama/dimension-adapters/master/fees/near-intents/index.ts)). Raising floors on SwapKit-class deals trades directly against the volume narrative used to justify the venue multiple; mix shift toward near.com (about a quarter of Q3 Intents volume, 100% fee direction by stated policy) and attachment creep on contestable public flow raise capture without a blunt take-rate fight ([Nansen NEAR Q3 2026](https://nansen.ai/post/near-q3-2026-report); [revenue.near.org](https://revenue.near.org/); [Evolving NEAR Tokenomics](https://www.near.org/blog/evolving-near-tokenomics)). Intents already dominates NEAR’s fee surface (~**87%** of DefiLlama-attributed fees on NEAR over 30 days); if this capture path fails, little else on the L1 replaces it.

## Confidential Mode widens the product and thins the proof

Confidential Intents executes size inside a permissioned NEAR private shard with a TEE bridge and no client ZK proofs — closer to venue-style dark execution than to Zcash-style anonymity, with destination settlement still public ([How Confidential Intents Works](https://www.near.org/blog/how-confidential-intents-works); [Aurora Confidential Intents](https://docs.intents.aurora.dev/confidential-intents)). NEAR frames the tradeoff explicitly as **trust-minimized rather than fully trustless** for UX and integrator adoption: **seven permissioned validators**, no public RPC, ingress sanctions screening, SHIELD as an application-layer pause/delay layer, and selective disclosure still on the roadmap ([NEAR Blockchain](https://www.near.org/blockchain); [How Confidential Intents Works](https://www.near.org/blog/how-confidential-intents-works); [Proactive Intents Security](https://docs.near-intents.org/security-compliance/proactive-intents-security)). Independent integrator notes have flagged unpublished TEE attestation detail and the absence of a fully public validator roster ([LeoDex editorial](https://leodex.io/editorial/near-confidential-intents-july-2026)). Contractually, confidential swaps carry no AppFee and no developer entitlement; Intents Technology “sets, charges, and retains” the Confidential Infrastructure Fee for its own account and may direct infrastructure fees to ecosystem recipients — a permission, not a public obligation to buybacks ([1Click Terms Schedule 1](https://docs.near-intents.org/security-compliance/terms-of-service)).

Adoption proxies are strong and dated. Nansen reports Confidential Intents GA after more than **$2.6B** in ZEC settled across Intents, Confidential TVL near **$160M** at Q3-end (up roughly **463%** QoQ), and near.com making confidentiality the default on **1 Sep 2026** while processing about **$2.57B** in Q3 — roughly one quarter of Intents volume ([Nansen NEAR Q3 2026](https://nansen.ai/post/near-q3-2026-report)). Early GA claims of ~**40–42%** confidential share on near.com before the default show revealed preference when UX friction is near zero ([GA announcement](https://www.near.org/blog/announcing-general-availability-of-confidential-intents)). Named institutional desk flow remains mostly marketing; the evidenced pattern is privacy-linked asset flow, sticky confidential balances, and default-driven mix. Size preference is plausible given whale-skewed public volume, but confidential-versus-public ticket distributions are not published.

For token capture, confidentiality splits the story: economic take *can* improve because partners have zero contractual claim on the confidential fee, while epistemic quality of the public proof deteriorates. Of issuance offset by revenue wallets in the window (**~15.8%**), only about **4.5** points trace to public collectors and about **11.3** points arrive as escrow releases — measured capture is a **range of roughly 4.5–15.8% of issuance** until per-stream attestation exists ([DefiLlama adapter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DefiLlama/dimension-adapters/master/fees/near-intents/index.ts)). Escrow unshields were about **$824K (~59%)** of buyback-wallet NEAR in the window; the same path feeds most of `fefundsadmin`. Escrow share of buyback NEAR has climbed through the confidential rollout. That is privacy working as designed, not missing money, and it is still a soft spot in any closed-loop “volume → scarcity” claim. Durability of the confidential wedge rests on liquidity primacy plus partner API embed plus a compliance-shaped trust model — not on cryptographic exclusivity peers cannot copy.

## Share came from embeds, not from vault security theater

Intents separates off-chain competitive fulfillment (solvers) from on-chain settlement on NEAR, with cross-chain signing via an **11-of-17** MPC/TEE network; many deposits still depend on PoA bridges while OmniBridge-style paths roll out chain by chain ([Chain Signatures in TEEs](https://www.near.org/blog/chain-signatures-tee); [HTW on NEAR Intents](https://www.htw.tech/blog/what-is-near-and-near-intents)). THORChain, Maya, and Chainflip instead hold native assets in L1-controlled threshold vaults underwritten by bonded validators — security as continuous bond-to-TVL underwriting rather than per-trade solver inventory ([THORChain Network Security](https://docs.thorchain.org/network-security-governance); [Chainflip Protocol Overview](https://docs.chainflip.io/protocol/protocol-overview); [Maya Security](https://docs.mayaprotocol.com/deep-dive/how-it-works/security)). Intents can add chains and long-tail assets without scaling a 2:1 economic bond; that flexibility is exactly where trust assumptions concentrate (PoA transit, upgradeable verifier, 1Click agent surface).

Wallets optimize for one integration, maximum pairs, and partner fee — not for bond-to-TVL bake-offs. The 1Click REST quote → deposit → status path collapses solver discovery into an embeddable API; asset coverage on the order of dozens of chains and hundreds of usable assets lets a single integration replace multiple vault venues ([1Click Swap API](https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/distribution-channels/1click-api/about-1click-api); [chain support](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/chain-support)). Brave Wallet’s Intents integration markets any-to-any coverage including BTC, SOL, and ZEC, not MPC versus TSS ([Brave Wallet NEAR Intents](https://brave.com/blog/near-intents/)). Against peers over 30 days to **2026-11-04**, Intents ran about **$4.0B** volume at **15.3 bp**, THORChain **$1.8B** at **12.3 bp**, Chainflip **$0.85B** at **31.5 bp**, and Maya **$0.53B** at **9.4 bp** — Intents prices above most peers while holding the majority share. DefiLlama “revenue” is not like-for-like across venues (NEAR wallet sweeps vs Maya’s 10% holder claim vs Chainflip burn), so volume leadership without capture-rate discipline overstates NEAR’s economic control ([DefiLlama NEAR Intents](https://defillama.com/protocol/near-intents); [Maya Protocol](https://defillama.com/protocol/maya-protocol); [Chainflip swapping](https://docs.chainflip.io/protocol/how-swapping-works)). Peer market-cap-to-revenue multiples are far tighter for THORChain and Chainflip than for NEAR on the same DefiLlama-style framing — volume share is not a cheap-revenue argument.

The credible threats are quote competition inside the same wallet pipe and meta-routers that treat settlement rails as interchangeable. THORChain’s REVSHARE subsidizes affiliates to fight for SwapKit order flow without raising the user fee; SwapKit already monetizes Intents beside THOR, Maya, Chainflip, and others ([THORChain ADR-029](https://blog.thorchain.org/adr-029-affiliate-revenue-share); [SwapKit Monetization](https://www.swapkit.dev/monetization/)). Across/UniswapX-style RFQ and ERC-7683 standardization raise the risk that Intents becomes one backend among backends ([cross-chain RFQ vs AMM](https://chainscorelabs.com/differences/chain-abstraction-and-intent-routing/cross-chain-routing-strategies/cross-chain-rfq-systems-vs-cross-chain-amm-routing-key-differences)). The right scoreboard for industry structure is peer volume share plus integrator concentration; the right scoreboard for the NEAR token claim is **captured bp**, capture share, and escrow attestation quality — not gross fees alone.

## Regulated wrappers sit small beside issuance and spot

The regulated NEAR stack is Bitwise-dominated and thin: US spot ETF NRR (0.75% fee, stakes in-house, retains ~67% of staking rewards after a 33% expense cut), Bitwise Europe’s staking ETP, smaller 21Shares and Virtune staking products, Grayscale’s tiny OTC trust (staking condition unmet as of mid-2026 filings), and Valour’s synthetic SEK tracker ([Bitwise NRR launch](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/newsroom/the-bitwise-near-etf-nrr-launches-as-first-spot-near-etp-in-the-us); [Bitwise Europe NEAR ETP](https://bitwiseinvestments.eu/products/bitwise-near-staking-etp/); [Grayscale Near Trust](https://www.grayscale.com/funds/grayscale-near-trust); [ETF Llama NEAR](https://etfllama.com/crypto/near-protocol)). Combined tracked AUM is about **$123M** — roughly **2.5%** of NEAR market cap — not a structural float vacuum. As of **2026-11-11**, NRR showed about **$55.86M** net assets, **$80.45M** cumulative net inflow, and **1.2%** of NEAR mcap after late-October and early-November outflows (including roughly **−$4.8M**, **−$3.6M**, and **−$0.77M** daily prints); assets near **$91M** on **21 Oct** then marked lower as price fell, so treating the AUM drop as pure “ETF rejection” overstates redemptions ([SoSoValue US NEAR spot](https://sosovalue.com/assets/etf/us-near-spot)).

In-house staking converts creations into bonded stake rather than exchange inventory, but NEAR’s unbonding of about **four epochs (~1–2 days)** makes the lock a soft float sink: outflows can recycle coins to venues quickly ([NEAR staking docs](https://docs.near.org/protocol/tutorials/staking-tokens); [Bitwise launch risks](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/newsroom/the-bitwise-near-etf-nrr-launches-as-first-spot-near-etp-in-the-us)). Sponsor fees paid in NEAR are a thin mechanical sell (~**$0.42M/year** at current NRR AUM) — second-order at today’s size. Mechanically, ETF creations are additive spot demand orthogonal to Intents fee capture; narratively, Bitwise sold NRR as an Intents/AI-agent proxy, so flows can trade as a sentiment ticker while Intents take remains the cash-flow engine ([Bitwise NRR launch](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/newsroom/the-bitwise-near-etf-nrr-launches-as-first-spot-near-etp-in-the-us)). Against ~**$112M**/day Binance spot, single-day flows in the low single millions are noise; launch-week prints near **$36M** cleared a higher bar that recent outflows did not. Capital-markets structure becomes first-order only if the physical stack scales toward high-single-digit percent of mcap or absorbs a large share of liquid supply for a sustained period — neither is true as of mid-November 2026. Do not model ETF AUM as evidence that Intents fee capture is working.

## What would change the view

What an RPC cannot show is the commercial map: **distribution owns most of the margin**, confidential rails can raise NEAR’s economic take while lowering public proof, competitive share is rented through wallet embeds that also host peers, and **ETFs are a small soft float overlay** plus a substitute story. The path that raises token capture without killing volume is attachment creep, near.com mix, and confidential growth **with** attestation that fees reach wallets — not a forced SwapKit 50/50. Treat Intents as a real fee engine whose link to NEAR scarcity is a **range with policy and partner risk**, not a closed loop.

Dated events and the attachment / issuance / inventory arithmetic that turns this map into a scoreboard are in [`docs/catalysts-and-path.md`](catalysts-and-path.md) (`data/derived/catalyst_math.json`, `data/derived/catalyst_calendar.json`). Attachment at **60%** with the **1.6%** cap puts issuance offset near **32%** without volume growth; inventory turns about **17×** per 30 days. Those are scenario arithmetic on committed metrics, not forecasts.

The fee waterfall that sits under those levers is in [`docs/fee-waterfall.md`](fee-waterfall.md): token-claimable take **~5 bp**, capture share **32.5%**, 1Click leakage **~10×**, AITR **~9.5 bp**, SwapKit’s explicit NEAR share of its partner fees **~0**. Venue-average capture masks pipe-level zero on the dominant aggregator.

The view weakens or breaks if any of the following arrive with evidence. Per-stream disclosure shows escrow unshields are not recurring fee revenue (one-offs, discretionary top-ups, or non-fee transfers), collapsing capture toward the public-collector floor near **4.5%** of issuance. SwapKit — or Ledger inside it — leaves or re-routes to THORChain/CEX, hitting the volume narrative and any hidden escrow-linked economics together. The share of volume with buyback fee entries rolls over, or new Commercial Agreements further zero out NEAR’s explicit line while partners keep **100+ bp**. Governance pauses buybacks, spends wallet NEAR on opex, or recycles holdings into liquid supply, breaking the holders-revenue interpretation DefiLlama has used since **2026-02-23**. Confidential Infrastructure Fee is retained by Intents Technology “for its own account” and **stops** being swept to NEAR revenue wallets — contractually allowed today. ZEC/privacy-led volume mean-reverts and whale cohort leaves, leaving low-fee stablecoin routes. Peers match confidentiality plus wallet distribution at lower all-in user cost, or meta-aggregators and standardized intent orders make NEAR settlement interchangeable. Intents remains a net NEAR exit venue and buybacks stay far below issuance even after any HSP-style cut — fee capture never becomes economically material to float. Until those falsifiers fire, the honest reading is a minority-but-rising NEAR claim on a distribution-owned fee engine, partially obscured by a permissioned confidential trust set, with capital-markets wrappers too small to rescue the multiple on their own.

## Distribution leverage (Ledger)

`ledgerswapkit.near` alone lists ~$2.01M partner fees / 30d — approximately **1.01×** NEAR’s captured Intents revenue in the same window. Leakage renegotiation is not a diffuse partner problem; it concentrates in a Ledger-class wallet. Exploratory; listed appfees ≠ settled NEAR capture.

# Can NEAR's Intents business grow into its dilution?

*A long-term investment thesis for NEAR: a token flow statement of what NEAR's value capture is today, what NEAR's price assumes it becomes, scenarios and a re-runnable projection to 2031 from the data, and the signposts that would tell them apart. Not investment advice. Window: the 30 days to 2026-11-04 for value capture, the 90 days 2026-08-07 → 11-04 for supply. Every number marked with an `n:` comment is recomputed from committed data by `make reproduce`.*

## Bottom line

**The long-term thesis (exploratory, chosen 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14, after the freeze).** NEAR's investment case rests on whether the fees NEAR captures from NEAR Intents can grow into the dilution its holders absorb, and on whether the price already assumes they will. Three findings, each recomputed by `make reproduce` ([section 6](#6-the-investment-case-what-the-price-assumes-what-the-evidence-supports-exploratory)):

- **If Intents' fees carried the price alone, it would assume fast growth.** For NEAR's price to rise 15<!--n:ic_base_r_pct-->% a year to 2031 and its market cap then to be 25<!--n:ic_base_x-->× captured revenue, that revenue must grow 84.9<!--n:ic_implied_cagr_r15_x25_pct-->% a year, from $19.6<!--n:ic_rev_90d_ann_m-->M to $424<!--n:ic_required_rev_2031_m-->M (54.0<!--n:ic_implied_cagr_r10_x50_pct-->% to 131.7<!--n:ic_implied_cagr_r20_x10_pct-->% across the grid of assumptions). The cross-chain venues tracked here grew 13.4<!--n:ic_market_cagr_1y_pct-->% last year, all of it Intents' own growth (the other venues' volume changed by -53.4<!--n:ic_market_ex_intents_cagr_1y_pct-->%), -1.9<!--n:ic_market_cagr_2y_pct-->% a year over two years and 47.1<!--n:ic_market_cagr_4y_pct-->% a year over four (15.4<!--n:ic_market_ex_intents_cagr_4y_pct-->% without Intents).
- **Today's fee structure does not get there at the market's recent pace.** With the market growing at its last-year pace and Intents' share at its twelve-month high, NEAR's take would have to rise from 4.13<!--n:ic_take_90d_bp--> bp to 37.4<!--n:ic_take_needed_bp--> bp of volume, 2.5<!--n:ic_take_needed_vs_gross_x-->× the whole gross fee Intents charges. - **Scenarios from the data.** Captured revenue would pay for 16.6<!--n:fwd_coverage_now_pct-->% of the dollar value of NEAR's dilution today (it is not all bought-back NEAR, and none of it is burned). On 2031-11-04, at a flat price, it would pay for 3.5<!--n:ic_bear_coverage_2031_pct-->% (bear), 44.6<!--n:ic_base_coverage_2031_pct-->% (base) or 277.5<!--n:ic_bull_coverage_2031_pct-->% (bull). Holding NEAR at today's price is a bet on more than Intents' fee capture: the L1's own value, a much larger cross-chain market, or a much higher take. ## The thesis

**Long-term claim (exploratory).** NEAR's token captures value from Intents only through the fees routed to NEAR's revenue wallets. Those fees would pay for a fraction of NEAR's dilution today, and if Intents carried NEAR's price alone, the price would assume they grow 84.9<!--n:ic_implied_cagr_r15_x25_pct-->% a year to 2031 (section 6.1), faster than the peer set's volume has grown over any window in the data (at most 76.9<!--n:ic_market_cagr_3y_pct-->% a year, over three years from the 2023 low). Section 6 sets the price against the evidence: the factors that would have to multiply, three scenarios, peers, a Monte Carlo and the signposts. What follows here is what is measured today.

NEAR's net issuance (minted minus burned) is about 88,295<!--n:net_issuance_near_per_day_window--> NEAR a day. Over the 30 days to 2026-11-04, the NEAR that verifiably arrived in the three wallets DefiLlama counts as NEAR Intents revenue was equal to **15.81<!--n:ladder_all_wallets_pct_of_issuance-->%** of that.

Only **4.53<!--n:ladder_public_collectors_pct_of_issuance-->** points of it can be traced on chain to public fee collectors. The other **11.25<!--n:ladder_escrow_unshields_pct_of_issuance-->** points arrived as releases from the Confidential Intents escrow (and 0.03<!--n:ladder_other_pct_of_issuance--> points from one other recurring source): the NEAR is there, but which fee stream paid for it is not visible from outside. The releases do move with confidential swap volume, day by day (section 2), which is what fees on those swaps would do. Of the 4.53<!--n:ladder_public_collectors_pct_of_issuance--> collector points, 0.56<!--n:ladder_collectors_escrow_funded_pct_of_issuance--> were fees paid from escrow-funded single-use accounts, which makes 11.81<!--n:ladder_escrow_all_pct_of_issuance--> points that passed through the escrow in all. ## 2. How we know it is the escrow, and what is actually missing (exploratory)

- **What an unshield looks like on chain.** A confidential withdrawal shows on chain as the escrow paying the exact amount to a fresh account, which then withdraws it to a revenue wallet. That is an observation: the documentation describes the release from the Treasury, not this pattern. In the window, all 909<!--n:escrow_funded_exact_30d_n--> such deliveries were funded by the escrow for the exact amount, to the yoctoNEAR (100.0<!--n:escrow_funded_exact_30d_pct-->%).
- **The escrow's identity is documented.** Account `51e8f94d…7cbe` is `PRIVATE_TREASURY_ACCOUNT_ID` in NEAR's Confidential Intents docs, "a production constant shared by all participants". The docs call it the Treasury and say it backs each confidential token one for one; "escrow" is our word for it.
- **Anyone can re-check it, and what the re-check does and does not show.** Every delivery is in `data/raw/escrow/deliveries.csv`; a funding transfer is there too for every single-use delivery in the 30-day window (909<!--n:escrow_single_use_30d_n-->) and for a traced sample before it. `python3 -m pipeline.fetch_escrow verify --all` re-reads every row from FastNEAR and archival RPC, with no key, and its output of 2026-11-08 is committed (`data/raw/escrow/verify_all.json`). Of the 3,597<!--n:escrow_recheck_rows_n--> rows, 3,587<!--n:escrow_recheck_rows_ok_n--> pass every check and the other 10<!--n:escrow_recheck_failed_n--> fail on block height alone, one to three blocks off (the table holds the block that includes the receipt; the check compares the block that executes it), so the command exits with status 1 on the committed data. All 1,051<!--n:escrow_recheck_funding_hops_n--> funding transfers pass, including all 909<!--n:escrow_recheck_window_funding_ok_n--> of the window's single-use deliveries. The 100.0<!--n:escrow_funded_exact_30d_pct-->% above tallies two columns of the table (`funder` and `exact_amount_flag`) over the window's single-use deliveries. Which deliveries are single-use does not depend on those columns (it follows from the committed BigQuery output of the arrivals), and the re-read confirmed the recorded funding transfer of every one of them on the chain. The SQL of the original funding scan is not in this repository, and its BigQuery job could not be retrieved on 2026-11-13. A new query, `sql/escrow_funding_scan.sql` (exploratory, 2026-11-13), re-derives the funding from the public dataset alone: it finds the same 1,262<!--n:escrow_rescan_deliveries_n--> deliveries in the window (all 1,262<!--n:escrow_rescan_table_rows_matched_n--> table rows match one), 909<!--n:escrow_rescan_single_use_n--> of them by an account that delivered once, and for 909<!--n:escrow_rescan_reproduced_n--> of those the same account, the same funding receipt, a funding of the exact amount, one funding only and no other inflow. So, for the window, the escrow rung no longer rests on uncommitted code. The code that assigned the table's path labels is still not committed, and before 2026-10-06 the funding hops are sampled. Details: [`docs/sources-checked.md`](docs/sources-checked.md), sections 2 and 3.
- **What is missing is attestation, not NEAR.** The NEAR verifiably arrived. Two candidate NEAR-side fee streams fit, and the public chain cannot split the 11.25<!--n:ladder_escrow_unshields_pct_of_issuance--> points between them:
  - **near.com's fees.** NEAR says near.com swaps "direct 100% of fees to a NEAR revenue wallet", without naming which. The same answer says swaps through third-party partners are "split 50/50 between NEAR and the partner"; the arithmetic in section 3 tests that. near.com's own code names three fee-related accounts, of which we discuss two (the third, `APP_FEE_DISTRIBUTOR`, is not discussed). `APP_FEE_RECIPIENT` (`176121bd…`) withdrew to the front-end fund (`fefundsadmin`) in every delivery of it in our table (2026-07-07 to 2026-10-21) and to the buyback wallet in none. `CONFIDENTIAL_FEE_RECIPIENT` (`60a0d43b…`) appears nowhere in FastNEAR's public index of the chain. Calling them the public-mode and confidential-mode fee accounts is our reading of those names.
  - **The Confidential Infrastructure Fee.** The 1Click terms (Schedule 1 §6.2, §6.4) say Intents Technology "sets, charges, and retains" it on confidential swaps, with no share to integrators. They state no rate ("if any", §6.3), and the public fee pages do not mention confidential swaps, so the terms say who would keep such a fee, not that one is charged. Schedule 1 §1.7 adds that infrastructure fees "may be directed by the 1Click Service to a designated fee recipient in connection with NEAR ecosystem infrastructure, development and growth activities", and names no recipient. Forwarding any of it to NEAR's wallets would be a choice, not a documented fact.
- **What the public side does show (exploratory, 2026-11-13).** Both candidates are fees on confidential swaps, and the releases behave like such fees. Daily escrow releases correlate 0.73<!--n:conf_rel_corr_solver--> with confidential solver volume (0.61<!--n:conf_rel_corr_solver_detrended--> with a 7-day trend removed, 0.59<!--n:conf_rel_corr_solver_dlog--> on day-over-day changes), and only 0.16<!--n:conf_rel_corr_oneclick--> with 1Click's public volume and 0.35<!--n:conf_rel_corr_bbfee--> with its listed buyback fee, which arrives through collector A instead. Releases ran at 14.8<!--n:conf_rel_bp_of_solver_window--> bp of solver volume over the window, with the buyback wallet getting a steady 1.11<!--n:conf_rel_bb_over_fe_oct--> to 1.34<!--n:conf_rel_bb_over_fe_window--> times the front-end fund's share. Near-intents.org's listed 0.2% fee fell from $4.07<!--n:conf_fe_listed_usd_per_day_pre-->K to $0.28<!--n:conf_fe_listed_usd_per_day_post-->K a day after 2026-08-28, while escrow releases to the front-end fund rose from $3.89<!--n:conf_fe_escrow_usd_per_day_pre-->K to $19.38<!--n:conf_fe_escrow_usd_per_day_post-->K. Releases that were one-off or discretionary would not track daily volume. This still does not say which fee each release is ([`docs/confidential-flow.md`](docs/confidential-flow.md)).
- **The falsifier.** A per-stream attestation by NEAR (which fees fund which escrow releases) would turn the range into one number. Nothing here alleges misconduct: this is consistent with a privacy feature working as documented, and the question is auditability. Since 2026-10-27 it may be the only route left: NEAR Intents' changelog says 1Click swaps now settle confidentially, so the public rung is expected to thin out (Limits).

## 6. The investment case: what the price assumes, what the evidence supports (exploratory)

*Chosen and built on 2026-11-13 (the Monte Carlo, 6.7) and 2026-11-14 (the rest), after the freeze. None of it changes a hypothesis, threshold, probability or the rating, or feeds anything they compute. Code: [`pipeline/investment_case.py`](pipeline/investment_case.py) and [`pipeline/model.py`](pipeline/model.py); method and recipes: [`docs/investment-case.md`](docs/investment-case.md); dashboard: the "Investment case" section of `site/index.html`.*

A long-term thesis has to say what the price already assumes, what the evidence supports, how far apart they are, and what would close the gap. This section does that in that order. Every figure is recomputed by `make reproduce` from committed data, and the two assumptions it needs (a required return and a revenue multiple) are shown as a grid, not chosen for the reader.

### 6.1 What the price assumes

### 6.2 What would have to multiply

Captured revenue = cross-chain swap volume × Intents' share × NEAR's take rate. To multiply by 21.6<!--n:ic_required_multiple_x--> by 2031:

- the market, at its last-year pace for five years, gives ×1.87<!--n:ic_mult_market_x-->;
- Intents' share, at its twelve-month high (78.6<!--n:ic_share_max_pct-->%, against 61.7<!--n:ic_share_90d_pct-->% over the last 90 days), gives ×1.27<!--n:ic_mult_share_x-->;
- which leaves ×9.1<!--n:ic_mult_take_x--> for the take rate: from 4.13<!--n:ic_take_90d_bp--> bp of volume to **37.4<!--n:ic_take_needed_bp--> bp, 2.5<!--n:ic_take_needed_vs_gross_x-->× the whole 15.2<!--n:ic_gross_bp_90d--> bp gross fee Intents charges today**.

Even if NEAR kept the whole gross fee, with Intents at its twelve-month-high share the market would have to grow 35.7<!--n:ic_market_cagr_needed_full_fee_pct-->% a year for five years. Fees are a policy choice, so this is not a proof that the take cannot rise; it is how far the current fee structure is from what the price would imply. Each factor is on DefiLlama's reported volume, which counts both legs of some swaps and, from 2026-09-01, more of Intents' volume ([`docs/leg-count.md`](docs/leg-count.md)); only the full product, the revenue, is unaffected. Because last year's market growth is all Intents' and partly that change, the market factor is generous and the take needed is, if anything, understated.

### 6.7 Uncertainty: the Monte Carlo

*Built on 2026-11-13; its forecasts M1-M4 (exploratory, dated after the freeze) are in `PREREG-ADDENDUM.md` (2026-11-13 23:50 UTC entry). Its main run is unchanged since; the block-bootstrap check and the run without the 2026-09 month were added on 2026-11-14.*

**The question** is the frozen rating rule's own: does captured Intents revenue reach the USD value of net new supply by 2031-11-04? The rule answers it with one constant price and one growth rate (G_req, 33.2<!--n:rating_g_req_pct-->% a year). The model answers it with a distribution.

**Coverage** is captured revenue over a trailing 90 days ÷ the USD value of the net new NEAR minted in the same 90 days, the quantity of P3. Today it is 16.6<!--n:fwd_coverage_now_pct-->% at one price (17.8<!--n:p3_quantity_90d_asof_pct-->% priced daily, as P3 measures it). Measured that way it has risen from 11.8<!--n:ic_cov_hist_first_pct-->% on 2026-02-04, the first date with 90 days of prices; the dashboard draws that history before the projection.

**How it is built.**
- *Supply:* the same daily path as G_req (realised share of the inflation cap, post-v87 burn), with HSP-029's ramp and without it.
- *Volume and price:* 10000<!--n:fwd_paths_n--> paths of 60<!--n:fwd_months_n--> months. Each month draws one of the 11<!--n:fwd_boot_months_n--> monthly changes in Intents volume (DefiLlama) and NEAR's mean price (CoinGecko) from 2025-12 to 2026-10, as a pair: the two move together (correlation 0.54<!--n:fwd_vol_price_corr-->), so they are not drawn separately.
- *Price drift removed:* NEAR's mean price rose 7.4<!--n:fwd_price_monthly_mean_pct-->% a month on average over those months. Replaying that for five years would assume the trend continues, so the main run keeps the price's volatility and its co-movement with volume but not its drift. The run with the drift is shown as a sensitivity. This choice was made after seeing a first run with the drift (disclosed in the addendum entry).
- *Take rate:* captured revenue ÷ volume over the last 90 days, 4.13<!--n:fwd_take_bp-->bp, held fixed. Volume keeps its sample drift, 0.8<!--n:fwd_vol_monthly_mean_pct-->% a month, all of which comes from one month (below).
- Standard library only, fixed seed: `make reproduce` gives the same numbers on every run.

### 6.9 The view, and what would change it

**The view.** Intents had the largest share of its peer set over the 30 days to 2026-11-04: 55.7<!--n:intents_share_peerset_30d_pct-->% of reported volume (39.7<!--n:legcount_peer_share_solver_lo_pct-->% to 41.2<!--n:legcount_peer_share_solver_hi_pct-->% on a one-sided basis), and its captured take has risen. In the scoring week that followed, THORChain's volume was larger (F4 missed at 37.2%; section 5). But as a claim on Intents' fees, NEAR is priced for more growth than the data show at today's fees: only the most favourable combinations (the three-year market pace from the 2023 low, or a 50× multiple) reach what the price would assume, the bull case on the four-year pace reaches about half of it, and in the base case captured revenue would pay for under half of NEAR's dilution in 2031. Holding NEAR at today's price is therefore a bet on more than Intents' fee capture: on the L1's own value, on a cross-chain market far larger than its history, or on a take rate well above today's gross fee.

**What would change it.** Toward the upside: NEAR's take rising toward what the price needs, the market growing faster than G_req for several years, or coverage running above the Monte Carlo's middle half (36.6<!--n:fwd_cov_2027_p75_pct-->% by 2027-11). Toward the downside: the take falling back toward its lowest post-switch month, Intents' share falling toward its twelve-month low, or NEAR's price rising without revenue. Section 5 lists the rest.

## Limits

- **Investment case (exploratory, 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14).** The required return and the 2031 revenue multiple are assumptions, shown as a grid. NEAR's market cap also prices the L1, which is not valued here, so the growth the price assumes is what Intents would need if it carried the price alone. The scenario anchors were chosen after the annual volumes, monthly shares and monthly take rates were computed, and the market history is one peer set whose members changed (THORChain alone before 2024); last year's growth of the set is all Intents' and partly DefiLlama's 2026-09-01 counting change. The Monte Carlo draws from eleven monthly pairs: no regime, trend in the take rate or mean reversion is modelled, the price drift is removed by a choice made after a first run, its volume drift comes from the one month that spans the counting change, and its coverage uses one price for the 90 days where P3 prices each day. We did not compare NEAR with other fee-switch or buyback tokens: it would need hand-picked external data. See section 6.

- **Second review (exploratory, 2026-11-12).** A later review re-ran parts of this analysis from the committed data. It found that the escrow share rose in a step at the end of August rather than as a trend, that the rise in the explicit fee follows a documented 1Click price change on 2026-09-03, that the front-end fund holds its 4.81<!--n:ladder_frontend_only_pct_of_issuance--> points of the ladder under its own five signers and has never forwarded NEAR to the buyback wallet, and that H1b's lower bound turns on single days. It changes no pre-registered result. Details, with a recipe for each figure: [`docs/second-review.md`](docs/second-review.md).
- **The public trail is shrinking.** NEAR Intents' changelog says that 1Click swaps settle confidentially from 2026-10-27. Collector B's daily transfers fell from 4,387<!--n:sr_collector_b_transfers_per_day_1006_1028--> on average over 10-06 to 10-28 to 109<!--n:sr_collector_b_transfers_1104--> on 11-04, and collector A's from 7,682<!--n:sr_collector_a_transfers_per_day_1006_1028--> to 2,739<!--n:sr_collector_a_transfers_1104-->. The 4.53<!--n:ladder_public_collectors_pct_of_issuance--> points traced to public collectors describe a settlement regime that is ending, and the per-swap method of [`docs/intents-fee-flows.md`](docs/intents-fee-flows.md) will not be repeatable on confidential swaps ([second review](docs/second-review.md), section 4). The fees themselves show it: by 11-04, 96.0<!--n:collector_a_escrow_usd_share_last_day_pct-->% of collector A's fee value came from escrow-funded accounts (section 1), so, if that share holds, a later 30-day window would show the collector rung mostly as escrow-funded rather than public (a forecast, not a measurement).

None of these can be computed from the committed data.
